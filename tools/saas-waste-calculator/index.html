How this calculator works

The number is built from three waste categories, each estimated from industry-average ratios and your own inputs:

  • Unused licenses - Your monthly spend × 12 × (100% minus your utilization input). If you spend $15k/month at 75% utilization, that's $45,000/year on seats nobody opens.
  • Overlapping tools - Number of overlapping tools × 70% of your average cost per tool. Overlap is rarely a full duplicate - usually 70% of one tool's spend could migrate to another you already pay for.
  • Auto-renewal exposure - Missed notice windows × average annual contract value × a 40% risk weight. Not every missed renewal is a total loss, but the exposure is the full contract value because you lost your leverage to renegotiate.

The estimate is deliberately conservative. A real audit - one that pulls actual contracts and real usage data - usually surfaces 20-40% more waste than this calculator shows, not less.

Frequently asked

How is SaaS waste calculated?

SaaS waste is the money spent on licenses that are not actively used, duplicate tools that overlap in function, and contracts that auto-renewed past the point of value. This calculator estimates each category using industry-average ratios: ~25% of seats are typically unused in mid-market SaaS stacks, roughly 1 in 4 tools overlaps meaningfully with another, and companies without a renewal process miss an average of 1-2 notice windows per year.

What is an acceptable SaaS utilization rate?

For most teams, aim for 80% or higher utilization on any paid seat. Below 70% is a renegotiation signal: at renewal, present the usage data and ask for right-sizing. Below 50% is almost always a case for cancelling the seats, not the vendor.

How often should I audit my SaaS stack?

Quarterly for fast-growing teams, twice a year for stable ones. The audit should cover active utilization (who is using what), contract status (what renews when), and overlap (which tools cover the same job).

What is a realistic savings target?

Mid-market teams that have never audited their SaaS stack typically find 15-25% savings in year one of structured management. That drops to 5-10% per year in steady state once the stack is right-sized.
