Where the money goes

Four ways the invoice quietly stops matching the contract.

None of these look like fraud. Each one looks like a slightly larger number on a bill that somebody already approved last month. That's exactly why they survive for years.

Pattern 01

The cap that wasn't honoured

You negotiated a 5% ceiling on annual increases. The renewal came through at 11%. Software prices rose 12.2% in 2026 against roughly 3% inflation, so CPI-linked caps are now routinely exceeded — and almost nobody goes back to check the clause they fought for.

Pattern 02

Seats that were never ordered

The order form says 142. Somebody provisioned 190 during a hiring push that stalled. Some vendors now actively audit seat counts and back-bill retroactively for provisioned-but-unused seats — a charge that arrives with no order form behind it.

Pattern 03

The tier you were moved into

Grandfathered plans get discontinued and customers are migrated to a replacement costing 30–200% more. AI features get bundled into a higher SKU and the tier you actually signed for quietly disappears. Your contract still names the old one.

Pattern 04

Line items that were already included

Support, onboarding, an integration, a sandbox — negotiated into the subscription fee during the deal, then invoiced separately eighteen months later by a billing system that never saw the redlines.

The process

Thirty minutes of your time. Fourteen days of ours.

You send two things

24 months of SaaS invoices — usually one export from your accounting system — and the contracts or order forms behind them. A shared Drive folder is fine. Missing contracts get flagged, not guessed at.

Questions

The things finance teams ask first.

How can a vendor overcharge us without anyone noticing?

Because the two documents never meet. The contract lives in a Drive folder owned by whoever ran the deal. The invoice goes straight to accounts payable, where it's checked against the previous invoice — not against the agreement. As long as this month looks roughly like last month, it gets paid. Overcharges survive as small monthly deltas that compound quietly across a full term.

What does it cost?

Nothing upfront. If we recover money, we take 25% of what actually lands in your account. If we recover nothing, you pay nothing and owe nothing. No retainer, no platform fee, no minimum term, no auto-renewal — which would be a poor look for us.

Won't disputing invoices damage our vendor relationships?

A dispute that cites the exact clause and the exact invoice line reads as a bookkeeping correction, not a confrontation. Vendors correct clear-cut errors quickly, because the alternative is a documented departure from their own contract. In comparable enterprise audits, roughly 80% of formally presented overcharges were credited back — and several providers were described by the auditors as cooperative once the error was laid out plainly.

Every letter goes out in your name and you approve each one before it's sent. If you'd rather not pursue a particular vendor, we drop it and it doesn't count toward the guarantee threshold.

How far back can we actually claim?

It depends on your contract, and this is one of the first things the audit tells you. Many SaaS agreements contain an invoice-acceptance clause limiting disputes to 30–90 days after the invoice date. Others are silent, which leaves the statutory limitation period. We map which of your contracts still have an open dispute window and which have closed, so you know what's genuinely claimable before anyone spends time on it.

How is this different from Zluri, Torii, Zylo or Spendflo?

Those platforms show you what you're spending and where it's concentrated. Useful work, and if you want app discovery, license reclamation and procurement workflow, they're better at it than we are. But none of them checks whether the amount charged was contractually permitted, and none of them recovers anything.

That gap shows up in the data: industry research puts recovery of identified software waste at only 5–15%, because finding a problem and fixing it are separate jobs requiring separate effort. We only do the second one.

What exactly do you need from us?

Two things. An export of 24 months of SaaS invoices from your accounting system, and the signed contracts or order forms that go with them — a shared Drive folder is the usual route. Most teams assemble both in about 30 minutes. Where a contract is missing, we flag the vendor rather than assume terms.

What happens to our contracts and invoices?

They stay yours. We work from a folder you control and you can revoke access at any point. We don't require an integration, an agent, or a browser extension, and we don't need admin access to any of your systems. If you ever want out, you take the folder and the ledger with you.

Why only eight audits a month?

Because a human reads every contract. Clause extraction gets us most of the way, but the judgment call about whether a charge is actually a breach — and whether it's worth disputing — isn't something we're willing to automate yet. When that changes, the number goes up.

Do you still do renewal tracking?

Yes, and it's free. Upload your contracts and we'll extract the auto-renew clause, calculate the notice deadline, and alert you before the cancellation window closes — unlimited vendors, no charge. It's how most people meet us. The audit is what we charge for.
