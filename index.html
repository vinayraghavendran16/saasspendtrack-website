Where the money goes

Four ways the invoice stops matching the contract.

None looks like fraud. Each looks like a slightly bigger number on a bill somebody already approved last month.

Pattern 01

The cap that wasn't honoured

You negotiated a 5% ceiling. The renewal came in at 11%. SaaS prices rose 12.2% in 2026 against 3% inflation, so caps get exceeded routinely — and nobody rereads the clause they fought for.

Pattern 02

Seats that were never ordered

The order form says 142. Someone provisioned 190 during a hiring push that stalled. Some vendors now back-bill retroactively for seats with no order form behind them.

Pattern 03

The tier you were moved into

Grandfathered plans get discontinued and customers migrated to replacements costing 30–200% more. Your contract still names the old one.

Pattern 04

Line items already included

Support, onboarding, a sandbox — negotiated into the subscription fee, then invoiced separately eighteen months later by a billing system that never saw the redlines.

Qualification

Worth doing for some companies. A waste of time for others.

The audit only pays for itself above a certain volume of contracted spend.

Send us your invoices if

  • You spend $500K+ a year on software
  • You have 150–2,000 employees
  • Most vendors are on signed contracts, not company cards
  • Somebody owns renewals, but nobody audits the bills
  • Your contracts contain uplift caps, seat commitments or negotiated inclusions

Don't bother if

  • Your SaaS spend is under $250K a year
  • Everything is month-to-month with no contracts
  • You have fewer than 15 paid vendors
  • You already reconcile contracts to invoices quarterly

Questions

What finance teams ask first.

How can a vendor overcharge us without anyone noticing?

The two documents never meet. The contract sits in a Drive folder owned by whoever ran the deal; the invoice goes to accounts payable, where it's checked against last month's invoice rather than the agreement. As long as this month looks like last month, it gets paid.

What does it cost?

Nothing upfront. We take 25% of what actually lands in your account. No recovery, no fee — and no retainer, no minimum term, no auto-renewal, which would be a poor look for us.

Won't disputing invoices damage our vendor relationships?

A dispute citing the exact clause and invoice line reads as a bookkeeping correction, not a confrontation. In comparable enterprise audits, roughly 80% of formally presented overcharges were credited back. Every letter goes out in your name and you approve it first — if you'd rather not pursue a vendor, we drop it.

How far back can we claim?

It depends on your contract, and this is one of the first things the audit tells you. Many agreements limit billing disputes to 30–90 days after the invoice date; others are silent. We map which of your contracts still have an open window before anyone spends time on them.

How is this different from Zluri, Torii or Spendflo?

They show you what you're spending, and they're better at app discovery and procurement workflow than we are. But none checks whether that spend was contractually permitted, and none recovers anything. Industry research puts recovery of identified software waste at only 5–15%, because finding a problem and fixing it are separate jobs.

What do you need from us?

24 months of invoices and the contracts behind them — usually one accounting export and a Drive folder, about 30 minutes of work. Where a contract is missing, we flag the vendor rather than assume terms.

What happens to our documents?

They stay yours. We work from a folder you control and can revoke at any point. No integration, no agent, no browser extension, no admin access to your systems. If you want out, you take the folder and the ledger with you.

Do you still do renewal tracking?

Yes, free. Upload your contracts and we extract the auto-renew clause, calculate the notice deadline and alert you before the window closes — unlimited vendors, no charge. The audit is what we charge for.
